Yes — you can absolutely build this, and your idea is more interesting than a normal “AI productivity app.”

What you are describing is essentially:

A personal AI companion that remembers what a person wants from life, converts dreams into achievable goals, turns goals into flexible daily actions, understands their thoughts and experiences over time, and continuously helps them stay on track without feeling like a strict task manager.

The important distinction is that you are not building just a todo app + chatbot. You're building a long-term personal goal operating system / AI life companion.

1. What you're actually building

I'd describe the product as:

An AI Goal Traveler

The user tells the AI:

“This is who I am, this is where I am now, and this is where I want to go.”

The AI then continuously works with the user to answer:

Where am I now?
What do I actually want?
Why do I want it?
Which dreams matter most?
What can realistically be achieved given my current situation?
What should I do today?
What did I learn?
What am I neglecting?
Why am I losing motivation?
Am I progressing?
What needs to change?
Where will I be in 3 months / 1 year if I continue?
What have I learned over the years?

And importantly:

The system should adapt to the person rather than forcing the person to adapt to the system.

That is probably the strongest idea in your entire concept.

23. Your principles are good, but formalize them

You wrote:

“Always be a good friend who supports him.”

I'd turn your philosophy into explicit AI behavioral rules.

Jarvis principles

1. Support, don't control

The user owns their decisions.

2. Challenge, don't shame

If they're avoiding something, call it out respectfully.

3. Encourage progress, not perfection

Missing one day isn't failure.

4. Protect the user's wellbeing

Don't optimize productivity at the expense of sleep, health or relationships.

5. Never manipulate

Motivation should not become emotional coercion.

6. Don't harm others

Never encourage achieving a goal through harming or exploiting someone else.

7. Be honest

If the AI doesn't know something, it should say so.

8. User autonomy

The AI recommends. The human decides.

Your “belief in something that helps you move forward” can remain non-religious and user-defined. Don't bake a particular spiritual/religious worldview into the product.

My assessment of your idea

I'd rate the concept itself very highly, but with one warning:

Don't build all of it at once.

Your idea currently contains approximately 4–6 products:

AI goal manager
AI personal assistant
Personal knowledge/memory system
AI journal

The opportunity is to make them feel like one product, not six disconnected features.
